# HfO2 manuscript: scientific acceptance and journal recommendation

Date: 2026-10-21. This is an internal assessment, not an editorial decision.

## Review setup

The fact base is the revised manuscript, the original/lower ordered atom
comparison, three-strain complete-path results and the final four crossed
local candidates. No new DFT was run for this assessment. The three reports
below differ only in emphasis; no reviewer identities are invented.

The local reviewer skill supplies a structured review process. Its Nature
importance threshold is not imposed as a JCTC requirement. The actual
[JCTC scope](https://researcher-resources.acs.org/publish/author_guidelines?coden=jctcce)
allows important applications as well as new methods, while excluding
straightforward applications of established methods to single-class systems.
A single material is not by itself disqualifying; the scientific increment
is the issue.

Shared claim: under one partial clamp, internal oxygen/Hf accommodation
changes the sampled mechanical response of preserving reversal relative
to phase escape. The lower candidates have a 0-to-1% penalty of 8.533
instead of 28.596 meV/HfO2. Matched releases and a -15.973 meV/HfO2 local
finite interaction connect retained patterns with permitted accommodation.
The final +1% q0 candidate lies 0.699 above the lower connected sampled peak.

## Reviewer 1: technical reliability emphasis

The declared finite comparisons are supported by common initial wells,
unchanged physical inputs, ordered trajectories, matched releases and an
actual reconnection to complete bands. They will interest researchers
comparing constrained transformation candidates. Atom-resolved Figure 4
makes the structural interpretation more accessible.

The principal technical limit is the distinction between measured local
candidates, sampled band peaks and a global barrier. Local searches retain
four observables and have no complete stability certificate; changing all
four does not isolate Qx alone. The 1--5 meV/HfO2 switching/escape gaps do
not resolve true ordering. These are limitations of stronger claims, not
automatic reasons to tighten every NEB threshold or compute every Hessian.

Recommendation posture: technically suitable for external assessment of
the bounded candidate/intervention results. Do not accept a global
path-selection or unique mode-energy claim from this evidence. Originality
and broader significance require the additional scrutiny below.

## Reviewer 2: originality and significance emphasis

The strongest increment is the common-boundary link between a mixed atomic
rearrangement, the smaller sampled strain penalty and competition with
escape from the same polar well. This is relevant to homogeneous hafnia
switching under mechanical constraints. The crossed states go beyond a
descriptive projection alone.

The [closest-source comparison](../benchmarks/hfo2_channels/20261008/mechanism_synthesis_20261021/literature_comparison.md)
also shows substantial prior coverage: boundary-sensitive pathways,
accompanying nonpolar reversals, lateral oxygen motion and shuffle--strain
relations are already known. Our original and lower preserving polygons
share lateral/non-through features; complete published endpoint/trajectory
identity has not been established. A more fully relaxed member of a known
route category remains a sufficient interpretation of the result.

Nonadditivity of four local candidate energies is useful evidence within
this study, but does not itself establish a new coupling theory or material
selection law. No additional predictive-model advantage was demonstrated.
Technical transparency and a readable figure improve trust, not priority.

Recommendation posture: the new data strengthen a material application
paper, but do not yet make a persuasive JCTC methodological or important-
application case. Treat JCTC as a high-risk target rather than a passed
scientific acceptance gate. This is the dominant concern.

## Reviewer 3: argument and readership emphasis

The revised argument now asks one material question and connects actual
atom motion to energies. The four approved Results sections remain;
the old two-coordinate release overview moves intact to in-article
Appendix F.3, Figure A9. The new main Figure 4 shows all twelve displacement
vectors, the three-strain candidate response and the final local states.
The abstract has four sentences and the Discussion interprets these results
instead of narrating the calculation history.

The likely readership is computational ferroelectrics and mechanically
constrained phase transformations. Cross-field impact remains less clear
because the study does not establish a transferable predictor or a new
path family. The known-route alternative must stay visible. The long
appendices are acceptable as evidence, but should not be the route by which
a reader first discovers the physical result.

Recommendation posture: the revised version is coherent enough for a
coauthor/external scientific read. Better expression does not resolve the
originality concern or extend the technical limits identified above.

## Cross-review synthesis and definite recommendation

The calculation protocol, analysis and revised research manuscript are
complete as finite deliverables. There is a defensible material result:
the examined candidate response depends strongly on which atomic
accommodation is reached under the same clamp, and the retained pattern
conditions the transverse/open-row response.

**Recommendation: do not treat the current manuscript as having passed a
JCTC submission-readiness gate, and do not make JCTC the default first
submission on the strength of this result alone.** It is a complete,
reviewable material-study draft. The decisive remaining risk is scientific
increment relative to known route classes, not missing software tests,
more polished contour plots, or a general lack of calculation volume.

The three emphases agree on the facts. They differ in weighting: technical
reliability permits the bounded statements; originality weighs against
JCTC readiness; readability has improved but cannot remove that risk.
No journal acceptance or rejection is predicted as fact.

The unresolved issue is now explicit and finite: a stronger JCTC claim
would need a distinct, testable scientific advance beyond recovering a
lower member of a known class. That cannot be obtained merely by repeatedly
rephrasing these same data. No new calculation matrix is authorized or
launched by this assessment. The current work is delivered with this
recommendation so that a completed task is not confused with journal readiness.
